## 1. Introduction and attribution policy

The main product photograph for every published catalog item is a B&H Photo Video product photograph selected by exact model, generation, variant and kit. The project owner reports that permission has been obtained to download, store, modify, convert and display these B&H photographs in this academic website. That permission is recorded in the manifest as a **permission basis**, not as a Creative Commons licence and not as a claim that B&H granted a general licence.

Previously verified Wikimedia Commons detail photographs are retained for cameras, lenses and one microphone where they already existed. Those photographs keep their original CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA records, creators and source pages. No licence was changed, removed or invented. No AI-generated image, generic substitute or similar-model substitution is used.

B&H photographs are identified by B&H product ID and product listing URL. The image file itself is stored locally, converted to WebP and displayed with a visible Photo Credits entry. B&H images are never labelled CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA.

Every main image is rendered on a **1200 × 1200** canvas with an approximately **1080 × 1080** content box, centred, with the original aspect ratio preserved. B&H originals are trimmed to the product bounding box before fitting; no stretching or product retouching is applied. Detail photographs retain their earlier 1200 × 1200 WebP processing.

## 8. Processing and verification

- Canvas: 1200 × 1200 WebP, quality 90.
- Content box: 1080 × 1080, aspect ratio preserved, Lanczos resampling.
- B&H originals: trimmed to the product bounding box with a 24 px pad; canvas background sampled from the photograph edge.
- Maximum upscale: 1.5×. Eighteen products used only a 1000 px or smaller B&H derivative and therefore have a small, recorded upscale; the rest are native or downscaled.
- Published main images: 80 / 80 decode at 1200 × 1200; SHA-256 recorded per file.
- Detail images: 85 / 85 retained and verified at 1200 × 1200.
- Every database image path resolves to a local file.
